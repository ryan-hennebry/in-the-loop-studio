Fetched from three `/ui/` articles on 2026-08-27 via WebFetch (all three succeeded).

- `agents-with-taste` - heading "Try it out", then a ~36 word paragraph, link text "Get the skill".
  This is the ONLY one with a dedicated closing section.
- `great-animations` - last heading is a content heading ("Great animations are hard"). CTA is
  appended to that section's final paragraph, link "Check out animations.dev".
- `building-a-drawer-component` - last heading "Getting creative", CTA appended, same link text.

**Two findings that correct the obvious reading of his layout.**

1. *A dedicated closing section is his exception, not his rule* (1 of 3). His default is to append
   the CTA to the last content section and mark the turn with PROSE, not space or a heading:
   "But if you'd want to learn more...", "We build this component (and more) together in my
   animations course."
2. *Every CTA offers an artifact; none asks for contact.* "Get the skill", "Check out
   animations.dev". The offer is what earns the line. A page with nothing to offer cannot copy the
   move directly, and a contact request dressed in his sentence shape reads as a pitch with nothing
   behind it.

**Why this matters here:** the recurring instinct on `/plan` is "the closing line needs more space".
The diagnosis is usually register, not distance - an unmarked pivot from third-person analysis to
second-person address. Emil never leaves that pivot unmarked, but he marks it with a heading or with
prose, never with a bespoke gap. A bare larger gap with no heading is something he does zero times.

**How to apply:** when a closing-line question comes up, ask what marks the register change before
reaching for space. See [[emil-kowalski-typography-reference]] for his measured intervals and
[[plan-closing-section-decision]] for what we proposed off the back of this.
